I am interested in looking at the intersection of social behavior and wealth on a global scale. For this example, I am using world Gallup polling data to the following question: “Have you helped a stranger in the past month?” I then compared these figures to a country’s GDP over a ten year span from 2015-2024.

Average percentage of willingness to help a stranger by year

This is how respondents answered whether they’ve helped a stranger in the past month on average from 2015-2024. Notice that there is a big shift between pre and post-covid, as 2020 is highlighted in red.

Average percentage of willingness to help a stranger among countries over time

This is a global visualization of how each country answered this question on average over the ten year span using a cool (and free) resource called Plotly. If you hover over each country, you should be able to see its affiliated percentage - and the globe spins!

Comparing highest and lowest GDP’s between 2015 and 2024

There is a clear shift when comparing 2015-2024 in the percentages that have helped strangers. In 2024, countries with the lowest GDP’s helped strangers much more on average than the highest GDP’s.

Comparing highest and lowest GDP’s during covid

This difference is interesingly the most dramatic during 2020 and the covid-19 pandemic.

This plot prints out the data for the highest and lowest ten GDP’s for each year and where they fall on the scale of helping strangers.

There is a significant negative relationship between GDP per capita and willingness to help a stranger:

For every $10,000 increase in GDP per capita, the percentage of people helping a stranger tends to drop by about 0.8%.

However, GDP explains only about 1.6% of the variation — so while the effect is real, it’s small.
